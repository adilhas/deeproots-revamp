# What is Web3?

## Introduction
The internet we know today was shaped by centralized companies.  
They own the data, control the platforms, and dictate the rules.  

**Web3 is the shift away from that control toward a decentralized, user-owned internet.**  
It’s not just a new technology — it’s a new internet model built on blockchain, governed by users, and designed for open participation.

## The Evolution of the Web
### Web 1.0 — Read Only
The early web (1990–2004) was static.  
You could read, but not interact. Pages were simple, and users were just consumers.  

### Web 2.0 — Read and Write
Web 2.0 (2004–present) brought platforms like Facebook, YouTube, and Twitter.  
You could create content, but companies owned your data and monetized your activity.  

### Web 3.0 — Read, Write, Own
Web3 changes the game.  
Now, users can own their identity, assets, and participation.  
With wallets, smart contracts, and tokens, the internet becomes open, programmable, and user-driven.  

## Core Principles of Web3
- **Decentralization** — No central authority. Control is shared across nodes and smart contracts.  
- **Permissionless** — Anyone can participate. No approvals, no gatekeepers.  
- **Trustless** — Code replaces middlemen. Smart contracts handle logic and security.  
- **Ownership** — Your tokens, your NFTs, your identity is truly yours, portable and protected.  
- **Native Payment** — Send and receive value with crypto, no banks required.  

## Why Web3 Matters
- **You own your data and assets** — not the platform.  
- **You earn and govern** — not just use.  
- **You participate globally** — no borders, no banks, no bias.  
- **You build and migrate freely** — your identity moves with you.
